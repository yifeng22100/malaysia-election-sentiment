Malaysia's rolling election cycle

Between Sabah's vote in November 2025 and the next general election due by early 2028, every Malaysian state assembly dissolves and votes on its own clock. This page tracks public, party and market sentiment as each one comes up — state by state, through to GE16.

What it means for GE16

Two states in, two losses for Pakatan Harapan — by different mechanisms, but the cumulative signal is now hard to wave away.

Johor and Negeri Sembilan broke against PH through genuinely different routes. Johor was a Johor-specific dynamic: PN sat out a third of the seats, and right-leaning Malay voters consolidated behind BN rather than splitting toward PH, handing BN a landslide that flattered BN more than it damaged PH on PH's own terms. Negeri Sembilan was structural: a formal BN–PN non-compete pact split all 36 seats between the two non-PH blocs and ran them as a single anti-PH front, converting a "genuinely uncertain" pre-election read into a clean 25-seat majority. Two different mechanisms, but PH lost both times — and in Negeri Sembilan it lost its own state chairman and DAP's secretary-general in the same night, a personnel cost Johor didn't carry.

That combination is what's driving the sharper national conversation now. A 48-seat Johor supermajority already strengthened UMNO's hand within BN; a second, pact-enabled win in Negeri Sembilan validates the BN–PN non-compete model as a repeatable template rather than a one-off — and gives both coalitions reason to consider extending it. For Anwar's unity government, the test was never really about vote share so much as whether PH could hold what it already governed; having now lost a state it held since 2018, plus two of its most senior figures personally, pressure for an early GE16 and for PH to rethink its own strategy will intensify. Melaka and Sarawak — both still undated — are the next data points, and now carry more weight than they would have a week ago.

What we're watching next
Watch: Melaka

With Johor and Negeri Sembilan both decided — and both breaking against PH — attention turns to Melaka, where Chief Minister Ab Rauf Yusoh said he'd set a dissolution date after weighing these two results, and to Sarawak, whose assembly must dissolve by 14 February 2027. Expect both timelines to firm up faster now that the BN–PN non-compete model has proven itself once.
