The Prime Ministers of Malaysia

Ten men have held the office since 1957. This page sets out what each is credited with and what each is criticised for — drawn from documented record, court judgments and mainstream reporting rather than commentary or forum opinion.

10 prime ministers since 1957 Static reference — not part of the daily auto-update

How this page is written

Every entry lists both achievements and criticisms, because every one of these administrations has both. Entries stick to matters of documented public record — legislation passed, policies implemented, court outcomes, election results, and events reported consistently across mainstream sources. Where a criticism is contested, it is stated as what critics argue rather than as settled fact. Convictions are described as what a court found. Nothing here is drawn from forum discussion, partisan commentary or unverified allegation.

In order of office
1 Tunku Abdul Rahman 1957–1970
Tunku Abdul Rahman Putra Al-Haj · UMNO / Alliance

Malaysia's founding prime minister, who negotiated independence from Britain and held the new country together through its formation and its first serious crisis. Known as Bapa Kemerdekaan — Father of Independence.

Recorded achievements
  • Negotiated and secured Malayan independence, declared 31 August 1957.
  • Built the Alliance — UMNO, MCA and MIC — establishing the communal power-sharing model that has shaped every Malaysian government since.
  • Oversaw the formation of Malaysia in 1963, bringing in Sabah, Sarawak and (briefly) Singapore.
  • Co-founded ASEAN in 1967, anchoring Malaysia in a regional bloc that still exists.
  • Governed in a deliberately multiracial style, positioning himself as a leader for all communities.
Criticisms & controversies
  • Presided over the separation of Singapore from Malaysia in 1965, a rupture he authorised and which remains contested.
  • His accommodationist approach to inter-ethnic economics was widely blamed by Malay nationalists for the grievances that fed into the 13 May 1969 riots.
  • His personal style — horse racing, golf, mahjong — drew sustained criticism from conservative UMNO figures who eventually manoeuvred him out.
  • Resigned in 1970 under internal party pressure following the 1969 riots and the suspension of Parliament.
2 Abdul Razak Hussein 1970–1976
Tun Abdul Razak bin Hussein · UMNO / Alliance → Barisan Nasional

Took office with Parliament still suspended after the 1969 riots, and used that moment to fundamentally restructure both the economy and the party system. Known as Bapa Pembangunan — Father of Development.

Recorded achievements
  • Introduced the New Economic Policy in 1971 — a 20-year programme to eradicate poverty and restructure economic imbalance between ethnic groups.
  • Restored parliamentary democracy in February 1971 after the National Operations Council interregnum.
  • Expanded the Alliance into the much broader Barisan Nasional coalition, absorbing former opposition parties including PAS.
  • Established diplomatic relations with China in 1974 — the first ASEAN leader to do so — and pursued a non-aligned foreign policy.
  • Authored the Razak Report 1956 as Education Minister, the foundation of the national education system.
Criticisms & controversies
  • The 1971 constitutional amendments made it seditious to publicly question 'sensitive issues' — including in Parliament — permanently narrowing political speech.
  • The NEP's ethnic quotas remain among the most contested policies in Malaysian public life, criticised for entrenching patronage and for outlasting its 1990 sunset.
  • Consolidating most opposition into BN substantially reduced meaningful parliamentary opposition for a generation.
  • Died in office in 1976, leaving the NEP's implementation to successors.
3 Hussein Onn 1976–1981
Tun Hussein bin Onn · UMNO / Barisan Nasional

A quieter, legalistic prime minister who inherited the NEP and focused on national unity and consolidating institutions. Known as Bapa Perpaduan — Father of Unity.

Recorded achievements
  • Continued NEP implementation with an emphasis on reducing inter-ethnic economic disparity.
  • Prioritised national unity as the organising theme of his administration.
  • Maintained political stability through a period of regional instability, including the Vietnamese refugee crisis.
  • Stepped down voluntarily in 1981 on health grounds, enabling an orderly transfer of power.
Criticisms & controversies
  • His administration is often characterised as cautious and lacking a distinct policy agenda of its own.
  • Presided over the 1977 Kelantan emergency, in which the federal government suspended the PAS-led state government and imposed direct rule.
  • Later broke publicly with his successor Mahathir and with UMNO's direction, reflecting unresolved tensions from his own term.
4 Mahathir Mohamad 1981–2003
Tun Dr Mahathir bin Mohamad · UMNO / Barisan Nasional

Malaysia's longest-serving prime minister at 22 years, and the most consequential and most divisive figure in its post-independence history. He industrialised the country and centralised power in roughly equal measure.

Recorded achievements
  • Drove rapid industrialisation and infrastructure development — KLCC and the Petronas Twin Towers, KLIA, the North–South Expressway, the Multimedia Super Corridor and Putrajaya.
  • Sustained average annual growth of roughly 6–7% across two decades, transforming Malaysia from a commodity economy into a manufacturing exporter.
  • Articulated Vision 2020, the goal of fully developed status by 2020, which framed national policy for a generation.
  • Rejected IMF prescriptions during the 1997–98 Asian Financial Crisis, imposing capital controls and pegging the ringgit — a heterodox response widely judged in hindsight to have worked.
  • Raised Malaysia's international profile as an assertive voice for the developing world.
Criticisms & controversies
  • Operation Lalang in October 1987: at least 106 people detained without trial under the ISA, including opposition leader Lim Kit Siang and civil-society figures; several newspapers had their licences suspended.
  • The 1988 judicial crisis: Lord President Salleh Abas and two Supreme Court judges were removed, and Article 121 was amended to subordinate judicial power to Parliament — an episode the Federal Court ruled unconstitutional in 2017.
  • Sacked and imprisoned his deputy Anwar Ibrahim in 1998 amid a policy dispute over the financial crisis; the prosecution triggered the Reformasi movement and was widely condemned internationally.
  • Presided over an expansion of money politics and patronage in UMNO, and of state-linked contracts awarded without open tender.
  • Curtailed the powers of the Rulers and of the press, and used the ISA against political opponents repeatedly across his term.
5 Abdullah Ahmad Badawi 2003–2009
Tun Abdullah bin Ahmad Badawi · UMNO / Barisan Nasional

Campaigned as a clean-government reformer after 22 years of Mahathir, won the largest mandate in Malaysian history, then lost BN's two-thirds majority four years later. Known as Pak Lah.

Recorded achievements
  • Led BN to its biggest-ever win in 2004 — 198 of 219 seats, over 90% of Parliament.
  • Transformed the Anti-Corruption Agency into the Malaysian Anti-Corruption Commission, with wider powers and greater institutional independence.
  • Launched the National Integrity Plan in 2004 and established a judicial appointments commission, partially addressing the legacy of the 1988 judicial crisis.
  • Allowed significantly greater press and political openness than his predecessor, including a freer internet.
  • Promoted Islam Hadhari, a framework intended to reconcile Islamic principles with modern, pluralist governance.
Criticisms & controversies
  • Presided over BN's loss of its two-thirds majority and five states in the 2008 'political tsunami' — its worst result since 1969.
  • Faced persistent allegations of nepotism centred on his son-in-law's political influence.
  • Used the ISA against HINDRAF leaders in 2007 despite his reformist positioning, damaging his credibility with Indian voters.
  • Criticised for administrative drift and for announcing more reform than he delivered; several signature projects stalled.
  • Forced out by his own party in 2009 following the 2008 election result.
6 Najib Razak 2009–2018
Dato' Sri Mohd Najib bin Abdul Razak · UMNO / Barisan Nasional

Son of the second prime minister, he pursued economic liberalisation and major infrastructure while presiding over the largest financial scandal in Malaysian history — and became the first former PM convicted and imprisoned.

Recorded achievements
  • Launched large infrastructure programmes including the Klang Valley MRT, the East Coast Rail Link and Tun Razak Exchange.
  • Repealed the Internal Security Act in 2012, ending indefinite detention without trial, and repealed the Banishment Act and Emergency Ordinance.
  • Introduced the 6% Goods and Services Tax in 2015, broadening a narrow revenue base — a reform many economists supported on fiscal grounds.
  • Introduced BR1M direct cash transfers to lower-income households, reaching millions of recipients.
  • Articulated the 1Malaysia framework and pursued closer economic ties with China.
Criticisms & controversies
  • The 1MDB scandal: over RM2.67 billion (about US$700 million) was traced into his personal accounts from the state fund, triggering investigations in at least six countries.
  • Convicted in 2020 on seven charges of abuse of power, criminal breach of trust and money laundering related to SRC International; the conviction was upheld by the Federal Court in 2022 and he began a 12-year sentence, later halved by the Pardons Board.
  • Replaced SOSMA for the ISA — retaining 28-day detention without charge and special trial procedures that rights groups said preserved much of the old regime.
  • Removed the Attorney-General and deputy prime minister who were investigating 1MDB, and suspended publications reporting on it.
  • Led BN to its first federal election defeat in 61 years in 2018, in a result widely read as a verdict on the scandal.
7 Mahathir Mohamad (second term) 2018–2020
Tun Dr Mahathir bin Mohamad · Bersatu / Pakatan Harapan

Returned at 92 to lead the opposition coalition that finally unseated the party he had run for 22 years — then resigned less than two years in, precipitating the collapse of his own government.

Recorded achievements
  • Led Pakatan Harapan to the first change of federal government in Malaysian history at GE14.
  • Abolished the deeply unpopular GST in September 2018, replacing it with the narrower SST.
  • Reopened the 1MDB investigations that had been shut down, leading directly to Najib's prosecution.
  • Became, at 92, the world's oldest serving head of government, and oversaw an initial period of significantly expanded press freedom.
Criticisms & controversies
  • Resigned in February 2020 amid a dispute over the promised handover to Anwar Ibrahim, directly precipitating the Sheraton Move and the fall of the PH government.
  • Failed to deliver several core PH manifesto commitments, including repeal of the Sedition Act and other restrictive laws.
  • Was criticised for reappointing figures associated with his earlier administration and for concentrating decision-making narrowly.
  • The unresolved succession question dominated his term and is widely regarded as the proximate cause of the coalition's collapse.
8 Muhyiddin Yassin 2020–2021
Tan Sri Muhyiddin bin Mohd Yassin · Bersatu / Perikatan Nasional

Became prime minister through parliamentary realignment rather than an election, and governed almost entirely through the COVID-19 pandemic with a wafer-thin majority.

Recorded achievements
  • Led the national COVID-19 response, including the Movement Control Order and one of the region's faster vaccine rollouts.
  • Introduced substantial pandemic economic support packages, including wage subsidies and loan moratoriums.
  • Maintained government functioning through an unprecedented public-health emergency despite a majority of only a handful of seats.
Criticisms & controversies
  • Took office via the Sheraton Move — defections that collapsed an elected government — without facing voters, a legitimacy problem that shadowed his entire term.
  • Advised the declaration of a state of emergency in January 2021 that suspended Parliament for roughly seven months, which critics argued was driven by his fragile majority rather than public-health need.
  • Faced sustained criticism for the size of his cabinet and for appointing political allies to government-linked company positions.
  • Resigned in August 2021 after losing majority support, becoming the shortest-serving prime minister in Malaysian history at the time.
9 Ismail Sabri Yaakob 2021–2022
Dato' Sri Ismail Sabri bin Yaakob · UMNO / Barisan Nasional

A caretaker-style prime minister of a fragile coalition who is chiefly remembered for a bipartisan agreement with the opposition that produced reforms neither side could have passed alone.

Recorded achievements
  • Signed a landmark memorandum of understanding with Pakatan Harapan in September 2021 — a formal confidence-and-supply arrangement unprecedented in Malaysian politics.
  • That MoU delivered the anti-hopping law, fast-tracked Undi18 implementation, and guaranteed parliamentary reforms including more opposition input.
  • Oversaw the reopening of the economy and borders as the pandemic eased.
  • Held the general election open rather than calling a snap poll during the pandemic, as agreed under the MoU.
Criticisms & controversies
  • Never led his party — he was UMNO vice-president while the party president remained outside government, leaving his authority persistently in question.
  • His government was widely seen as unstable and transitional, dependent on both UMNO factional politics and opposition forbearance.
  • Called the November 2022 election during the monsoon season, drawing criticism after flooding affected polling in several areas.
  • Led BN to the worst result in its history at GE15 — 30 seats — and left office after just 14 months.
10 Anwar Ibrahim 2022–present
Dato' Seri Anwar bin Ibrahim · PKR / Pakatan Harapan (unity government)

Appointed by the King after Malaysia's first hung parliament, ending a political journey that ran from deputy prime minister to prisoner and back over 24 years. Leads a broad unity government spanning former rivals.

Recorded achievements
  • Formed a stable government from the first hung parliament in Malaysian history, ending a period of four prime ministers in as many years.
  • Holds the finance portfolio alongside the premiership and has pursued targeted subsidy reform and fiscal consolidation.
  • Presided over a period of comparatively low unemployment and strong approved-investment figures following the pandemic.
  • Restored a measure of political stability, and secured a confidence vote and anti-hopping protections that have held the coalition together.
  • Reintroduced parliamentary reforms including select committee scrutiny and a commitment to separating the Attorney-General and Public Prosecutor roles.
Criticisms & controversies
  • Governs in coalition with UMNO — the party that prosecuted and jailed him — which reform-minded supporters view as a compromise of PKR's founding platform.
  • Has not delivered several long-promised institutional reforms, including repeal of the Sedition Act and SOSMA, and the AG/Public Prosecutor separation remains incomplete.
  • Subsidy rationalisation and the expanded SST have drawn criticism over cost-of-living pressure.
  • His coalition has lost ground in consecutive state elections — including Johor and Negeri Sembilan in 2026 — amid an erosion of both Malay and, more recently, non-Malay support.
  • Critics point to continued use of investigative and regulatory powers against political opponents and online speech.
