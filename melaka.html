Melaka: waiting on Johor and Negeri Sembilan's lessons

Chief Minister Ab Rauf Yusoh has said he'll set a dissolution date only after weighing how Johor and Negeri Sembilan landed — and now that both have, a state government coalition wobble in July gives the decision extra edge.

Reading the mood

Melaka was supposed to be the simplest remaining question — now it isn't quite.

For most of 2026, Melaka's election was framed as a pure timing question: the assembly's term runs out on 27 December 2026, requiring a poll by February 2027 at the latest, and speculation had ranged from an April dissolution and June poll to a later window. Chief Minister Ab Rauf Yusoh repeatedly signalled he'd rather watch how Johor and Negeri Sembilan played out before committing to a date — both have now happened, both broke against Pakatan Harapan, which removes his stated reason for waiting and raises the likelihood of a dissolution announcement soon.

What complicates the picture is a coalition wobble that has nothing to do with election timing directly: in mid-July, DAP quit Melaka's state government over a bill allowing the appointment of up to seven nominated assemblymen, a move DAP objected to on democratic-legitimacy grounds. Ab Rauf maintained the government remains stable despite the walkout. It's a reminder that Melaka's unity-era BN-PH arrangement, like Negeri Sembilan's before its election, isn't free of internal friction — worth watching for whether it affects how (or whether) BN and PH contest the state together when a date is finally set.
