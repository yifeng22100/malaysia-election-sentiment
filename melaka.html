Melaka: dissolved, dated, and still sorting out its alliances

The assembly was dissolved on 23 September and the Election Commission has set nomination for 31 October and polling for 14 November. On 20 October the Registrar of Societies deregistered Perikatan Nasional, and two days later the High Court stayed that decision, so PN can contest. On 21 October BN and PAS settled a 21–7 seat split, and they plan to unveil their candidates together.

The current assembly

The assembly was dissolved on 23 September 2026. Until results come in on 14 November, this is the composition as the 2021 Melaka state election left it.

BN returned to power with a two-thirds majority after the state government collapsed in October 2021, triggering the snap poll.

Reading the mood

The timing question is settled. What's still open is who runs with whom.

Ab Rauf Yusoh announced the dissolution on 22 September, after Governor Tun Mohd Ali Rustam consented, and it took effect the next day. That was three months before the assembly's term would have run out on 27 December. On 7 October the Election Commission set nomination for 31 October, early voting for 10 November and polling for 14 November. There are 687,697 registered voters, 271 polling centres and an estimated cost of RM28.45 million. BN goes in defending the 21 seats it won in 2021, and the BN–PN non-compete model from Johor and Negeri Sembilan is the obvious template. But BN delayed formal talks after dissolution, and PH kept saying it was open to a deal. Analysts read both moves as leverage over PAS's seat demands. On 10 October PAS president Abdul Hadi Awang said the two sides had agreed to cooperate in principle, with seat numbers and candidates still under discussion. PN deputy chairman Tuan Ibrahim Tuan Man said PN wants "more than four" of the 28 seats. On 21 October Zahid Hamidi said the talks were finalised: BN contests 21 seats under its own logo and PAS and its allies take the other seven.

Hanging over the talks is Perikatan Nasional's own legal status. On 22 September, after a complaint from Bersatu, the Registrar of Societies issued PN a show-cause notice asking why it should not be deregistered, and suspended its activities from 23 September, the day Melaka dissolved. PN called the action malicious and on 5 October filed for judicial review, asking among other things whether it can field candidates in Melaka. BN chairman Ahmad Zahid Hamidi has said PN's components will likely contest under their own logos. On 20 October the Registrar deregistered PN, saying its reply to the show-cause notice had not refuted the grounds. On 22 October the High Court stayed both the deregistration and the suspension until PN's judicial review is decided, which leaves PN free to contest Melaka under its own banner. The court has not yet ruled on whether RoS acted lawfully.

Background: for most of 2026, Melaka's election was framed as a pure timing question: the assembly's term runs out on 27 December 2026, requiring a poll by February 2027 at the latest, and speculation had ranged from an April dissolution and June poll to a later window. Chief Minister Ab Rauf Yusoh repeatedly signalled he'd rather watch how Johor and Negeri Sembilan played out before committing to a date — both have now happened, both broke against Pakatan Harapan, which removes his stated reason for waiting and raises the likelihood of a dissolution announcement soon.

What complicates the picture is a coalition wobble that has nothing to do with election timing directly: in mid-July, DAP quit Melaka's state government over a bill allowing the appointment of up to seven nominated assemblymen, a move DAP objected to on democratic-legitimacy grounds. Ab Rauf maintained the government remains stable despite the walkout. It's a reminder that Melaka's unity-era BN-PH arrangement, like Negeri Sembilan's before its election, isn't free of internal friction — worth watching for whether it affects how (or whether) BN and PH contest the state together when a date is finally set.

22 Oct
Coalition

High Court stays PN's deregistration and suspension pending judicial review, so Perikatan can contest Melaka under its own banner

Two days after the Registrar of Societies cancelled PN's registration, Justice Azizan Md Arshad of the Kuala Lumpur High Court stayed both the deregistration and the 23 September order suspending PN's activities. The stay runs until the court decides PN's judicial review and carries no order as to costs. The judge found "extraordinary circumstances" in PN's political role and its members' rights as elected representatives and state governments. He said the respondents suffer no prejudice because PN's activities can still be monitored. He did not rule on whether RoS acted validly. Senior federal counsel Nurhafizza Azizan argued that the original application was academic because PN had not yet been deregistered when it was filed, and that PN should file a fresh judicial review. PN's counsel Azhar Azizan Harun argued that RoS acted early, two days before the 30-day period in its own show-cause notice ran out. PN chairman Ahmad Samsuri Mokhtar welcomed the ruling and rallied the coalition's machinery for the campaign. Neither the government nor RoS has said publicly whether it will appeal. With nomination nine days away, PN's parties can now consider standing under the PN logo again, rather than only their own symbols or a borrowed one.

20 Oct
Coalition

RoS deregisters Perikatan Nasional eleven days before nomination; Takiyuddin says PN's parties haven't chosen a logo and points to PH's 2018 use of the PKR symbol

The Registrar of Societies announced on 20 October that it has cancelled PN's registration under subparagraph 13(1)(c)(iv) of the Societies Act 1966. It said PN's reply to the 22 September show-cause notice, submitted on 12 October, did not refute the grounds for deregistration or justify keeping the coalition registered. The case began with a complaint from Bersatu, received on 11 August, that it had been left out of a PN Supreme Council meeting on 16 July and that its membership status was in question. Speaking at Parliament the same day, PN secretary-general Takiyuddin Hassan said the parties formerly under PN (PAS, Wawasan, Gerakan, MIPP and Pejuang) have not yet decided which logo to use in Melaka. "There is still plenty of time for us to decide which seats we will contest," he said. He pointed out that in 2018 the PH parties all contested under PKR's logo, and said even parties that aren't registered as established political parties can still field candidates. The High Court has given PN leave for judicial review but refused an interim stay of the suspension order. Its application for a stay will be heard on 22 October. "We are also defending the rights of the people of Melaka who want Perikatan Nasional," Takiyuddin said.

Ab Rauf Yusoh signalled the Melaka state election would likely be held after the Johor and Negeri Sembilan polls concluded, saying he would review both results before deciding on a dissolution date.

Malay Mail

20 Feb
Early speculation

Early 2026 reporting flagged an April dissolution, June poll window

Ahead of firmer signals from the Chief Minister, initial speculation in local media suggested the assembly could dissolve as early as April 2026 with a state poll by June — a window that passed without action as the CM opted to wait on Johor and Negeri Sembilan instead.

31 Oct: nomination day · 14 Nov: polling day
