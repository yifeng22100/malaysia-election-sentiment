Negeri Sembilan: the pact holds, PH's leaders fall

The BN–PN non-compete pact delivered exactly what the late polling suggested — a combined majority — but the scale of it, and who lost their own seat, is the real story: DAP secretary-general Anthony Loke and sitting Menteri Besar Aminuddin Harun were both defeated.

The 16th Negeri Sembilan State Assembly as elected on 1 August 2026. Neither BN nor PN reached the 19-seat majority alone — their combined 25 seats under the non-compete pact is what forms the government.

Reading the mood

The result wasn't the surprise — Ong Kian Ming's seat model had BN-PN at 23–25 a week out. The surprise is that the pact's own top two rivals in PH, Loke and the sitting MB, lost their personal seats.

Decisive
Sentiment read: the BN–PN non-compete pact converted a "genuinely uncertain" pre-election read into a clear, if narrower-than-Johor, mandate — and PH's own leadership losses make it feel more emphatic than the topline seat count alone suggests.
UncertainDecisive

The 23 July Vodus poll had PH ahead on vote share (42% to BN's 35%) with the seat count too close to call. What actually decided the race was the mechanism analysts had flagged all along: the BN–PN non-compete pact meant the anti-PH vote was never split between two rival Malay-nationalist options the way Johor's PN-sits-out dynamic worked — BN and PN simply divided the state's 36 seats between themselves (25 to BN, 11 to PN) and ran as a single anti-PH front everywhere. BN converted 18 of its 25, PN converted 7 of its 11, for a combined 25-seat, two-thirds-plus majority. Bersatu's decision to break from that pact and contest all 24 of its own seats independently — the strategy born out of February's party purge — returned nothing.

The detail driving the "shock" framing in same-night coverage wasn't the seat count but two names: DAP secretary-general Anthony Loke lost Chennah, and Menteri Besar Aminuddin Harun — in office since 2018 — lost Linggi. Losing a sitting Menteri Besar's own seat is rare in Malaysian state politics; losing the federal cabinet minister who runs one of PH's two anchor parties in the same night compounds it. PH's own election director, Selangor Menteri Besar Amirudin Shari, attributed the defeat to rivals' "identity politics" and said the coalition would recalibrate its messaging before GE16. Aminuddin's own concession was more self-critical: "Now Malaysians have to think: do they want racial rhetoric or development? … Maybe my report card is still lacking."

"A loss here would sharpen pressure for an early general election and accelerate the realignment already under way inside the unity government" — this is exactly the outcome pre-election analysis flagged as the more consequential test of the two, and it has now happened. — East Asia Forum and Malay Mail pre-election framing, late Jun–Jul 2026, evaluated against the confirmed 1 Aug result

The Undang succession dispute over the Yang di-Pertuan Besar — Tuanku Muhriz versus the Undang-proclaimed rival claimant Tunku Nadzaruddin — remains unresolved and legally separate from the election outcome; the Court of Appeal stayed a contempt bid tied to the case just two days before polling, with the underlying dispute still pending. It did not appear to meaningfully sway the vote either way, based on coverage so far, but it hands Negeri Sembilan's incoming BN–PN state government an unfinished constitutional question alongside its new legislative majority.

Governance

MB weighs deputy exco posts to accommodate BN, PAS and Wawasan

Ismail Lasim said the state government is considering creating deputy executive councillor positions — a first for Negeri Sembilan — to fit in more elected representatives from the BN-PN governing pact, including PAS and Parti Wawasan Negara, while keeping the full exco capped at 10 members. Governance

Ismail Lasim targets Friday for finalised 10-member Exco

New Menteri Besar Ismail Lasim said the executive council lineup should be finalised by Friday (7 Aug) at the latest, and that a 10-member council — the size previously used in the state — would be most suitable. He plans to meet Umno president Ahmad Zahid Hamidi to discuss the composition, including whether to appoint PN assemblymen to the exco, before the proposed lineup goes to the Yang di-Pertuan Besar for approval.

Government

Ismail Lasim sworn in as new Menteri Besar

The seven-term Juasseh assemblyman and Negeri Sembilan Umno information chief, 67, took his oath before the Yang di-Pertuan Besar, Tuanku Muhriz Tuanku Munawir, at Istana Besar Seri Menanti, succeeding the defeated Aminuddin Harun. Ismail previously sat on the state Exco from 2004–2018 overseeing education; BN said Exco portfolio announcements would follow in the coming days.

Reaction

BN-PN eyes Melaka next; MIC and MIPP both win zero seats

BN chairman Ahmad Zahid Hamidi said the two coalitions "will have follow-up discussions... on the formation of the new state government," with PH's Amirudin Shari naming Melaka — where BN already holds 21 of 28 seats — as the next stage of the cycle. Separately, final vote-share figures confirm MIC (2.17%) and PN-aligned MIPP (0.97%) both contested and won no seats, leaving the incoming BN–PN government without a single elected Indian representative for the first time.

1 Aug
Reaction

PH blames "identity politics," Aminuddin concedes with self-criticism

PH election director Amirudin Shari said rivals' use of identity politics cost the coalition the state and that PH will realign its messaging ahead of GE16. Outgoing Menteri Besar Aminuddin Harun, defeated in his own seat of Linggi, said voters now have to decide "racial rhetoric or development," accepted the result, and said his eight years in office may still have fallen short: "maybe my report card is still lacking."

Free Malaysia Today

1 Aug
Result

BN–PN pact wins 25 of 36 seats; Loke and Aminuddin both lose their seats

Official BERNAMA tally: BN 18, PN 7 (combined 25), PH 11 — the BN–PN non-compete pact's first electoral test, ending PH's two-term hold on the state. The bigger shock was personal: DAP secretary-general Anthony Loke lost Chennah and sitting Menteri Besar Aminuddin Harun lost Linggi, both to BN-PN candidates. Bersatu's independent 24-seat run, mounted after February's party purge, won zero seats. Results were declared from 8:27pm.

BERNAMA · Free Malaysia Today · Malay Mail · Malaysiakini

1 Aug
Polling day

All 401 polling centres open; EC expects initial results by 10pm

Voting opened at 8am across 401 centres and 1,668 streams for 859,760 registered voters (including 7,391 postal voters), closing at 6pm. The Election Commission said it expects initial results as early as 10pm and full results before midnight, continuing to target 72–75% overall turnout after early voting hit 91.06%. Afternoon thunderstorms were forecast across most of the state.

The Edge Malaysia · BERNAMA

31 Jul
Campaign trail

Campaign closes with final push from Anwar, Zahid, Muhyiddin

The 14-day campaign period ended at 11.59pm. PM Anwar Ibrahim attended a Kuala Pilah religious programme, BN chairman Ahmad Zahid Hamidi and deputy Mohamad Hasan campaigned across constituencies, and Bersatu's Muhyiddin Yassin made a final push of his own. Caretaker MB Aminuddin Harun stressed face-to-face voter contact over social media, and candidates urged outstation residents to return home to vote, with cost of living, jobs, water supply and housing the recurring doorstep issues.

The appellate court fixed 30 July for case management to set a hearing date, pausing the contempt track two days before polling.

Polling Professional research

First published poll: Vodus has PH ahead but short of a majority

A Vodus Research survey (fieldwork 9–21 Jul, n=437) put PH's popular vote at 42% to BN's 35% and PN's 7%, projecting PH 17 seats, BN 15, PN 2 and two too-close-to-call — short of the 19-seat threshold either way. Economic stakes

Analysts flag risk to the state's tech-hub investment push

Negeri Sembilan drew RM19.1 billion in approved investment across 295 projects last year — nearly triple 2024 — on the back of the Malaysia Vision Valley 2.0, NS Semiconductor Valley and Nilai photonics-AI manufacturing projects. SCMP reports existing projects aren't expected to flee over one state poll, but the political uncertainty could slow future investment commitments and reshape the policy environment they depend on.

Fresh off its landslide, BN figures are publicly framing Negeri Sembilan as a different kind of contest requiring a different approach — a tacit acknowledgment that the Johor result (built on a Malay-vote consolidation PN inadvertently enabled by sitting out seats) may not repeat itself in a state with tighter margins and a live three-way fight.

DAP's central executive committee has told its Negeri Sembilan machinery — including secretary-general Anthony Loke's own seat — to avoid the combative "bury BN" framing critics blamed for part of Johor's swing. Seat lineup

BN–PN seat-sharing pact finalised: 25 seats to BN, 11 to PN

BN will field 25 candidates — 16 UMNO, seven MCA, two MIC — while PN allocates its 11 to PAS, Gerakan, MIPP and Wawasan. PH is contesting all 36 seats alone. Smaller parties ASLI and PSM are fielding one candidate each; Berjasa is expected to field seven; MUDA, Pejuang and Bersama have all opted out of this race entirely.

What we're watching next
Watch: Melaka

With Johor and Negeri Sembilan both now decided — and both breaking against Pakatan Harapan — attention shifts to Melaka, where Chief Minister Ab Rauf Yusoh has said he'll set a dissolution date after weighing these two results, and to Sarawak's assembly, which must dissolve by 14 February 2027. Two results in a row favouring the BN–PN axis, plus the loss of PH's own state leadership in Negeri Sembilan, will intensify scrutiny of Anwar's unity government ahead of GE16.
