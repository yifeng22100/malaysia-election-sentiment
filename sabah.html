Sabah: GRS holds on, Warisan roars back

The state that opened the 2025–2028 election cycle delivered the messiest result of it so far — a hung assembly, a DAP wipeout, and a Warisan comeback — before GRS built a working majority and settled back into government.

Reading the mood

Sabah politics ran true to form: fragmented, personality-driven, and settled by coalition arithmetic after polling day rather than on it.

Settled, after a messy start
Sentiment read: no outright majority on election night, but GRS converted its plurality into a stable, multi-party government within days — this state's political story is now the calmest of the three decided so far.
UncertainDecisive

No coalition reached the 37-seat majority line on 29 November, but GRS's 29 seats gave incumbent Chief Minister Hajiji Noor — who defended his own Sulaman seat — the strongest hand in post-election talks. A cabinet was sworn in on 1 December 2025 built around GRS plus BN (6), UPKO (3), STAR (2) and KDM (1), comfortably past the majority threshold, with Joachim Gunsalam, Masidi Manjun and Ewon Benedick installed as deputy chief ministers. The government has since branded its second term "SMJ 2.0," a continuation of the Hala Tuju Sabah Maju Jaya development agenda from Hajiji's first term.

The more talked-about story was the opposition, not the government. Shafie Apdal's Warisan more than doubled its prior showing to 25 seats, re-establishing itself as Sabah's dominant opposition force and Shafie's strongest personal result since losing power in 2020. DAP, by contrast, was wiped out entirely — a stark result given the party's continued relevance in federal government — underlining how disconnected Sabah's political alignments are from the Peninsula's BN/PH/PN framing. Analysts have since described Sabah as increasingly a genuine three-way contest between GRS, Warisan and a diminished BN, rather than an extension of any peninsular coalition's fortunes.

"Political realignment and governance challenges in Sabah"

A SEDAR Institute analysis published months after the government's formation assessed GRS's multi-party cabinet as functionally stable but structurally fragile — a coalition of convenience spanning parties with distinct local bases, dependent on continued federal goodwill and oil-royalty negotiations to hold together through the term.

Government

Second Hajiji cabinet sworn in with three deputy chief ministers

GRS formed government with BN, UPKO, STAR and KDM, comfortably clearing the 37-seat majority line. Joachim Gunsalam (PBS), Masidi Manjun (Gagasan) and Ewon Benedick (UPKO) were named deputy chief ministers, with the cabinet drawing from GRS, Gagasan, PBS, LDP, Sabah UMNO, PBRS, UPKO, STAR, KDM and PKR.

30 Nov 2025
Result

Hung assembly: GRS 29, Warisan 25, DAP wiped out

Official count across 73 of 79 seats: GRS 29, Warisan 25, BN 6, UPKO 3, Independents 5, STAR 2, PH 1, PN 1, KDM 1 — no coalition reached the 37-seat majority outright. Hajiji Noor held his Sulaman seat; DAP failed to win any seat, a marked reversal from its previous Sabah presence.
