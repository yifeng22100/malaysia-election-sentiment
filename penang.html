Lim Guan Eng escalates public feud with Chow over retaining BN in government

GE16 positioning

Penang PH holds Aug 8 post-mortem, retains BN exco member, sets up election sub-committee

Reacting to BN-PN's landslide gains in Johor and Negeri Sembilan, Penang PH chairman Chow Kon Yeow convened an Aug 8 meeting to review the results and Umno's continuing exco seat in the Penang state government, alongside a new sub-committee to coordinate election preparedness — constituency-level analysis, stronger social media outreach and Undi18-voter engagement. Chow said the coalition would stay the course on inclusive, development-focused governance rather than pivot to a race-based platform, and confirmed the BN exco member would be retained through the next general election.

Exco removal

Exco member Fahmi Zainol charged with domestic assault, suspended, then removed

Fahmi Zainol was alleged to have assaulted his wife at their Bayan Lepas home on 22 February 2026; he was charged in court on 27 February with voluntarily causing hurt and pleaded not guilty. PKR suspended him from all party posts around 1–2 March, and Chief Minister Chow Kon Yeow terminated him as state exco effective 4 March. Bukit Tambun assemblyman Goh Choon Aik was appointed to replace him days later, taking the Trade and Social Unity portfolio.

By-election

PN routs PH in the Sungai Bakap by-election, an early warning sign

Perikatan Nasional's Abidin Ismail beat PH's Joohari Ariffin by 4,267 votes — a sharply increased majority versus the 2023 general election — after Chinese and Indian turnout fell 10–13 points. Chow Kon Yeow attributed the swing to cost-of-living pressure, diesel-subsidy rationalisation and unmet water-supply promises. It did not change the assembly's overall balance but was widely read as early erosion of non-Malay support for PH.

Context

Penang has been governed by DAP-led administrations continuously since 2008, first under Lim Guan Eng and since 2018 under Chow Kon Yeow. As Malaysia's semiconductor and electronics manufacturing hub, the state's economic profile gives its politics an outsized national profile relative to its size.

No election has been called and none is expected imminently — this state's term does not require a dissolution until 29 Aug 2028 at the earliest edge of its constitutional window. See the Overview page for how this fits into the wider 2025–2028 state election cycle, and the Parties page for background on PH-DAP.
