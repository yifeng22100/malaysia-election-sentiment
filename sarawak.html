Sarawak: the question isn't if, it's how many seats

GPS's sec-general says polls are "definitely" happening this year, Premier Abang Johari alone controls the timing, and the real open question is procedural: will the assembly's approved expansion from 82 to 99 seats be ready in time, or will Sarawak vote on its old boundaries once more?

The current assembly

Sarawak has not voted in this cycle. Its assembly still sits as the 2021 Sarawak state election left it.

GPS took 76 of 82 seats — the assembly having expanded from 71 to 82 for this election — one of the most lopsided results anywhere in Malaysia.

Reading the mood

Sarawak's politics run on a different clock from the Peninsula's — insulated from the BN/PH/PN framing, and this time complicated by its own procedural drama.

GPS Secretary-General Fadillah Yusof said in January 2026 that Sarawak's state election is "definitely" happening this year, though the exact date remains entirely at Premier Abang Johari's discretion under the state constitution — the assembly must dissolve by 14 February 2027 at the latest, with a poll due within 60 days after. GPS has publicly urged its own machinery to "go for an A," a confident framing consistent with the coalition's dominant position since it split from federal Barisan Nasional in 2018 to govern Sarawak independently.

The distinctive wrinkle this cycle is seats, not swing. The Sarawak assembly has already approved expanding the DUN from 82 to 99 seats, but that expansion needs Dewan Rakyat endorsement and an Election Commission redelineation process to actually take effect — and as of July 2026, Abang Johari himself acknowledged the election may have to proceed on the current 82-seat map if the redelineation isn't finalised in time. Either way, GPS says it expects to hold a strong majority; the real uncertainty is procedural and timeline-driven rather than a genuine contest for power.

5–9 Aug
Redelineation

Cabinet push for the 17 new seats, but a minister says they may not be ready in time

Sarawak ministers Nancy Shukri and Alexander Nanta Linggi raised the stalled 82-to-99 seat redelineation at Cabinet, and GPS secretary-general Fadillah Yusof said the coming election should include the 17 new seats. But a minister cautioned the proposal — detailed in a leaked Election Commission document dated 25 June — may not be ready before Sarawak next goes to the polls. Communications Minister Fahmi Fadzil said Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim has instructed the EC to submit its latest report and that the federal government is "committed" to completing the expansion before the next state polls, without giving a date.

Federal politics

GPS minister disputes claim East Malaysia is losing its "kingmaker" role

Responding to PBM president and Julau MP Larry Sng's claim that a consolidating BN-PN pact could win a simple federal majority after GE16 without needing Sabah or Sarawak's blocs, Sarawak Tourism Minister Abdul Karim Rahman Hamzah said GPS and GRS "will continue to be relevant in federal politics" and questioned whether Peninsula-based coalitions would really forgo cooperation with the states that hold most of Malaysia's natural resources. The exchange lands as Sarawak's own election approaches, with GPS's federal leverage now an explicit part of the campaign backdrop.

Timing

Abang Johari still won't name a date: "some say October, some say November"
